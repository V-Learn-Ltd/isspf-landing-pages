Overview

Your keeper walks out ready, and you know what the half hour before kick-off is for.

Match day is a different job from the training week. In the week, training should be complex, because that's where your keeper learns. Before kick-off, you do the opposite: a warm-up set up so he gets a lot of success, and a clear job for each situation this opponent is likely to create.

This course shows you how goalkeeper coaches who've worked in the professional game run that half hour, and how they plan the week that leads up to it.

Who it's for

Anyone who coaches adult goalkeepers, at any level:

  • the volunteer who looks after the keeper at a Sunday-league or amateur club
  • the goalkeeper coach at a semi-professional club
  • the specialist at a professional one

You might see your keeper once a week or every day. Either way, you're usually there on match day, and that's where this course starts.

"Senior Pro Level" describes the course, not your club. Coach mostly young keepers? The Pro-Youth course is the one for you.

Outcomes

A warm-up with a purpose. You'll run the half hour before kick-off so your keeper walks out feeling ready.

A plan for every opponent. You'll turn what you know about the next team, however little, into a few plain jobs your keeper can take onto the pitch.

A training week he learns from. You'll plan sessions complex enough to teach him to read the game, with the high-intensity work early so he's fresh for the match.

An answer when a session falls flat. You'll know what each session is there to do, so you'll know what to change.

A keeper looked after as a whole. You'll cover fuel, pressure and setbacks such as losing his place, with specialists in each.

How is the course delivered?
Through video lectures from every goalkeeping coach and specialist who teaches on it, with reading references before and after each one. It's fully online, with no cohorts and no live classes.
Can I fit it round a season?
Yes. It's self-paced, on any device, so you can pause a module and pick it up when your fixtures allow.
Do I need a science background?
No. You'll hear coaches explaining their reasoning about coaching, and you work through it on your own.
What order do the modules run in?
They open in order, each one after a short test on the module before.
Is it in my language?
The lectures are in English, with subtitles in English, Spanish, French, Dutch, German and Italian.
How is it assessed?
A short multiple-choice test after each lecture, and a tutor-assessed assignment that checks it's landed. You're awarded a certificate when you reach the 75% pass grade.

Frequently asked questions

Is it only for coaches at professional clubs?
No. It's for anyone who coaches adult goalkeepers, at any level. "Senior" means adult keepers, and "Senior Pro Level" is the level of the course, not a test of your club. The examples in the course come from the professional game, and what carries across is the shape of the job: every keeper warms up, every match has an opponent, and every week has at least one session before it.
I'm a volunteer and see my keeper once a week. Is it worth it?
Yes. Match day is the other time you're with him, so the half hour before kick-off counts for more, not less. You'll also see how to coach your keeper inside the small-sided games you already run with the team, so your one session goes further.
What if I know very little about the opponent?
Use what you have, even if it's a result and what you saw last time you played them. The less you know, the fewer jobs you give him, and the more the warm-up does the work. The idea stays the same at every level: whatever you know about how they score becomes a job for your keeper.
Is it all about match day?
No. Match day is where the course starts, and the lectures go well past it. You'll plan the training week, look at whether a drill trains your keeper's reading of the game, judge his decisions fairly, and help him through pressure and setbacks such as losing his place.
Will it help us win?
No course can promise a result, and this one doesn't. A keeper can do everything asked of him and still concede, or get it wrong and get away with it. What the course gives you is a way to check the match against the jobs you set, as well as the score, so you know whether your coaching did its part.
Is it a coaching licence, or a degree?
Neither. It's a CPD-certified course that you'd hold alongside your UEFA, FA or federation licence, not instead of it. "Masters" names the course's level. It isn't an MSc or any other university qualification. If it isn't right for you, you're covered by a 30-day money-back guarantee.
