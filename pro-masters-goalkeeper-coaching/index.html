Why do I need this?

Most goalkeeper coaches learn the job a piece at a time: YouTube clips, other coaches' sessions, their own experience in goal. Each piece helps. What's often missing is how the pieces fit together, from the first session to the match.

  • A drill comes without its reason. A clip shows a keeper diving and catching. It doesn't show whether they're reading the play, or where the drill fits in your training.
  • Clean handling that fades in the match. If your keeper always knows where the ball is going, they never practise reading it. In a match they have to read first. The course shows you how to train that.
  • Training isn't built for the next opponent. The sessions are good, but none is planned around how the next opponent creates chances.
  • The rest of the job needs a plan too. Pressure, losing their place, fuel for training and set plays all shape a keeper's matches. Each has its own module here.
  • The why isn't always clear. A goal goes in and you wonder why your keeper started from there. So do they, and so might the head coach. "Decision-making" doesn't answer it.

This course covers all of it, in order. You get drills and sessions from goalkeeping coaches and specialists, each with its reason. So you know why you're doing what you're doing, and you send your keeper out ready. You can explain it to your keeper and the rest of the team, and to other coaches when you need to.

Overview

You coach two different jobs, training and match day. This course covers both.

In training, sessions should be complex, because that's where your keeper learns. Before kick-off, you do the opposite: a warm-up built to send your keeper out confident, and a clear job for each situation this opponent is likely to create.

Training is also where decisions are built. Plenty of drills work on the save. This course trains what comes before it too: what your keeper looks at as play develops, where they stand and which action they choose.

You'll see it done. A UEFA A goalkeeper coach walks through real sessions planned around two matches. There's a session for each way the next opponent scores, and crosses worked against opposition and tackle bags because the league is physical. After the match, the coach checks whether the aims of those sessions showed up.

Another module uses GPS data to show keepers do more explosive work in training than in matches. That's why the hardest sessions come well before the match.

You learn it from goalkeeping coaches and specialists who've worked in the professional game, with the drills, the sessions and the reason behind each one.

Anyone who coaches senior goalkeepers, at any level:

Outcomes

  • Decisions trained with the technique. Your sessions sharpen how your keeper reads play, takes up position and picks an action, so their technique holds up in matches.
  • Training with a purpose. Every session has a reason, and the hardest ones come well before the match, so your keeper arrives fresh.
  • A plan for every opponent. You'll turn what you know about the next team, however little, into a few clear jobs for your keeper.
  • A match day you've prepared for. You'll have a warm-up that sends your keeper out confident, and a plan for the set plays they'll face.
  • A fair look at the match. You'll review the match against the job you gave your keeper, and judge their decisions by what they could see.
  • Support beyond the pitch. You'll cover nutrition, pressure and setbacks such as losing their place, including how to explain a selection to a keeper you've left out.

Frequently asked questions

Is it only for coaches at professional clubs?

No. It's for anyone who coaches senior goalkeepers, at any level. "Senior" means adult keepers, and "Senior Pro Level" is the level of the course. The examples come from the professional game, and they carry across because the job has the same shape everywhere: every keeper trains, every match has an opponent, and every keeper warms up.

I'm a volunteer and see my keeper once a week. Is it worth it?

Yes. With one session, every minute counts, so you'll learn to train decisions and technique together. You'll also see how to coach your keeper inside the team's small-sided games. And match day gives you a second chance to prepare them.

Can't I find this on YouTube?

Clips are useful for ideas. The course gives you what they leave out: where a drill belongs in your sessions, what the next opponent changes, and how to run match day. The drills and sessions come from goalkeeping coaches and specialists, each with its reason, in the order you'll use them.

What if I know very little about the opponent?

Use what you have, even if it's a result and what you saw last time. The less you know, the fewer jobs you give your keeper. Whatever you know about how they score becomes one of those jobs.

What does each environment cover?

The training environment comes first. It covers the coach's role, what a match asks of a keeper, how to train decisions and technique together, planning sessions, nutrition and pressure. The game environment covers decisions and anticipation, real sessions planned around two matches and the next opponent, the warm-up, set plays, setbacks, match analysis and training load. Match day sits in the game environment, with the sessions before it.

When will I see it in my coaching?

From your next session. The methods come from goalkeeping coaches and specialists in the professional game. What you learn goes straight into your next sessions, your next opponent plan and your next warm-up.

Do I need a science background?

No. It's built for coaches. Goalkeeping coaches and specialists explain their reasoning in football terms, and you work at your own pace.

How does the course work?

You work through video modules from each coach and specialist, with reading references before and after each one. It's all online, so you study when and where suits you.

Can I fit it round a season?

Yes. It's self-paced and works on any device, so you can pause a module and pick it up when your fixtures allow.

What order do the modules run in?

They open in order, each after a short test on the one before. Training comes first, then the game.

Is it in my language?

The modules are in English. You can switch on subtitles in English, Spanish, French, Dutch, German or Italian.

How is it assessed?

A short multiple-choice test after each module, and a tutor-assessed assignment. You're awarded a certificate when you reach the 75% pass grade.

How does it sit with my coaching licence?

You hold it as a professional certificate next to your UEFA, FA or federation licence. "Masters" names the level of the course. See which football associations and universities recognise ISSPF courses

What if it isn't right for me?

You're covered by a 30-day money-back guarantee. See the refund terms.
