Why do I need this?

If you coach adult goalkeepers, you've probably pieced your coaching together yourself. There's no shortage of drills. What's missing is a plan that links each session to your keeper's next match.

  • Drills arrive without their day. Clips, reels and other coaches' sessions show you a drill, but not which day it belongs on or what it's building towards.
  • Good sessions don't add up to a match. Your keeper can have a run of sessions that each went well and were never built around the team they're about to face.
  • A flat session leaves you guessing. Without knowing what a session was for, you're left guessing what to change it to.
  • Someone always asks why. The head coach stops the video on a goal and asks why your keeper started from there. Something about decision-making doesn't answer it.
  • The warm-up turns into one more session. It's a natural habit from the training week, but match day asks for a keeper who walks out ready.

This course gives you the whole plan: drills and sessions from professional goalkeeping coaches, with the reasoning behind each day, so you send your keeper out ready and can explain every session to anyone who asks.

This course enables you to:

  • Run a match warm-up that gives your keeper a lot of success, so they walk out ready
  • Coach the decisions a keeper makes before the save: what to look at as play develops, where to stand and which action to choose, as well as the technique of the stop
  • Give your keeper a job for each situation the next opponent is likely to create
  • Build the training week from how the next opponent scores
  • Put the high-intensity work early in the week, so your keeper arrives fresh on match day
  • Know what each session is for, so you know what to change when one falls flat
  • Design drills and sessions that make your keeper read the game, as well as repeat a movement
  • Judge your keeper's decisions by what they could see at the time, not by what you saw from the side
  • Prepare your keeper for set plays, and organise the defence against them
  • Support your keeper through pressure, setbacks and what they eat in a match week

Overview

Your keeper walks out ready, and you know what the half hour before kick-off is for.

Match day is a different job from the training week. In the week, training should be complex, because that's where your keeper learns. Before kick-off, you do the opposite: a warm-up set up so your keeper gets a lot of success, and a clear job for each situation this opponent is likely to create.

The training week is also where decisions are built. Most drills and tutorials teach the technique of the save. This course trains what comes before it too: what your keeper looks at as play develops, where they stand, and which action they choose.

You'll learn it all from goalkeeping coaches who've worked in the professional game, with the drills, the sessions and the reasoning behind each one.

Who it's for

Anyone who coaches adult goalkeepers, at any level:

  • the volunteer who looks after the keeper at a Sunday-league or amateur club
  • the goalkeeper coach at a semi-professional club
  • the specialist at a professional one

You might see your keeper once a week or every day. Either way, you're usually there on match day, and that's where this course starts.

Outcomes

  • A warm-up with a purpose. You'll run the half hour before kick-off so your keeper walks out feeling ready.
  • Decisions trained before the save. Your sessions will build how your keeper reads play, takes up position and picks the action, alongside the technique of the stop.
  • A plan for every opponent. You'll turn what you know about the next team, however little, into a few plain jobs your keeper can take onto the pitch.
  • A training week your keeper learns from. You'll plan sessions that teach your keeper to read the game, with the high-intensity work early so they're fresh for the match.
  • An answer when a session falls flat. You'll know what each session is there to do, so you'll know what to change.
  • Your keeper supported as a whole. You'll cover fuel, pressure and setbacks such as losing their place, with specialists in each.

Frequently asked questions

Is it only for coaches at professional clubs?

It's for anyone who coaches adult goalkeepers, at any level. "Senior" means adult keepers, and "Senior Pro Level" is the level of the course. The examples come from the professional game, and they carry across because the shape of the job is the same everywhere: every keeper warms up, every match has an opponent, and every week has at least one session before it.

I'm a volunteer and see my keeper once a week. Is it worth it?

Yes. Match day is the other time you're with your keeper, so the half hour before kick-off counts for even more. You'll also see how to coach your keeper inside the small-sided games you already run with the team, so your one session goes further.

What if I know very little about the opponent?

Use what you have, even if it's a result and what you saw last time you played them. The less you know, the fewer jobs you give your keeper, and the more the warm-up does the work. Whatever you know about how they score becomes a job for your keeper.

Is it all about match day?

Match day is where the course starts, and the modules go well past it. You'll plan the training week, build sessions that train your keeper's decisions, judge those decisions fairly, and help your keeper through pressure and setbacks such as losing their place.

How soon will I see a difference?

From your next match day. You'll be coaching with the methods of goalkeeping coaches and specialists whose roles have included Brentford, Stoke City, Red Bull Salzburg, Dinamo Zagreb, Real Valladolid and the Estonian Football Association. What you learn goes straight into your next warm-up, your next opponent plan and your next training week. Every session gets a clear purpose, and your keeper develops at their own pace.
