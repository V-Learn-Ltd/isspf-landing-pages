Overview

Know what each young goalkeeper you coach should be working on, how to move him on, and how to build skills that hold up in his matches.

The course covers the whole job: your role as his coach, his long-term development, how skills are learned and progressed, when he trains alone and when with the team, distribution and playing out, handling pressure, analysis, and nutrition as he grows. Every lecture is about the decisions you make with the keepers in front of you, whether that's one keeper in your child's team or an academy squad.

Anyone can show you the drill. We make sure you can explain it.

Who it's for

  • You coach young goalkeepers, in your child's team, across a club's age groups or in an academy.
  • You've got plenty of drills, and you want to know which one this keeper needs next.
  • You want his training to show up in his matches.

It works one keeper and one skill at a time, so it fits a single keeper as well as a squad.

Outcomes

  • A plan for every keeper. You'll know what each young keeper should be working on, whether you coach one keeper or a squad.
  • Clear signals for moving him on. How often he gets it right tells you whether to stay with the basics, add the finer detail or bring the game in.
  • Skills that hold up in matches. Each skill moves from the basics into game situations a piece at a time, so his training looks more like his matches.
  • A keeper who plays his part in the team. He knows his job when his team has the ball and when it hasn't, and he can play out under pressure.
  • An answer when anyone asks why. When he, a parent or a head coach asks why he's working on something, you can say where that skill is and what comes next.

Course modules

Ten lectures from seven professional goalkeeping coaches and two performance specialists.

Skill Acquisition in Goalkeeping: Developing technical proficiency for situational effectiveness

Daniel Tumelty-BevanRoles have included head of academy goalkeeping at Birmingham City

How a keeper learns a new skill, and when repetition helps or practice should look like the game. You'll learn to use how often he gets it right as your signal to move him on.

Individual development of the goalkeeper within a team environment

Ivan KovčićRoles have included heading Dinamo Zagreb's academy goalkeeping programme

When a keeper trains on his own and when he trains with the team, and how to spot his problems through football situations. You'll also get a simple test: work on what keeps happening, not on a one-off.

Goalkeeper Distribution in Academy Practice and Game Situations: FC Barcelona

Dani OrtizRoles have included coaching goalkeepers in FC Barcelona's academy

How the academy builds distribution: the keeper and his outfielders beating a high press together, the weaker foot as a real option, and keepers rating their own passes like a traffic light. You'll see the same steps applied to one skill.

Managing Pressure: Emotion, Anxiety and Coping, Part A

Jeff RichardsonRoles have included academy goalkeeping coach at Brentford

Emotion, anxiety, how a keeper sees a big moment, and how to prepare mentally before a game. For the keeper who's sharp in training and different on match day.

Goalkeeping Expectations: Roles of the GK within the function of the game

Jeff Richardson

The keeper's job when his team attacks, defends and transitions, plus communication and the key situations he has to deal with.

Goalkeeper Coaching Analysis: Developmental Approach to Goalkeeping

Graeme SmithPlayed the game professionally

The types of goalkeeper analysis used in developmental football, and how they shape a goalkeeping curriculum. One example looks like a keeper beaten by a header, until the footage shows the cause was his positioning and his judgement of the cross.

The role of the goalkeeper coach

Fraser StewartRoles have included head of goalkeeping at Kilmarnock and coach education at the Scottish FA

What it means to be a specialist goalkeeping coach, and what you need to consider in training and in the game.

Youth Goalkeeper Development: Long Term Development

Eduardo CachuloRoles have included NEOM's goalkeeping academy and Real Valladolid's senior goalkeeping

The goalkeeper coach's place in a club's technical department, the markers of success in the position, and the move from youth to senior football.

Training Design & Application in Youth Development (LTAD)

Alex Segovia VilchezRoles have included leading performance at Singapore's national development centre

A development model for physical qualities, and how exercise progresses through youth development.

Nutritional Support for the Youth Soccer Player: Maturation Status & LTAD

Jack ChristopherRoles have included head of performance support in Chelsea's academy

How nutrition supports a young player through his development, as he grows and matures.

LTAD stands for long-term athletic development: how a young player's training builds over the years.

Frequently asked questions

I coach my son's team, with one keeper. Is this for me?

Yes. It's for anyone who coaches young goalkeepers, from a parent with one keeper to an academy squad. The coaches and specialists who teach it have worked at professional clubs and in national programmes, but what they teach follows one keeper and one skill at a time. So it fits a single keeper as well as a group, at any club.

I'm not a sports scientist. Will I follow it?

Yes. It's taught by goalkeeping coaches and performance specialists, about the decisions you make with your keepers. The lectures on pressure and nutrition sit alongside the goalkeeping ones: pressure shapes how a keeper performs on match day, and nutrition supports him as he grows. If you can watch a keeper and count what he gets right, you can use it.

Isn't there plenty of this on YouTube?

There are plenty of drills, and many are good. A clip shows a drill, but it can't tell you whether your keeper is ready for it yet. That call is what the course teaches. You'll see sessions in the lectures, and the drills themselves are easy to find elsewhere.

How soon will I see a difference in my keeper?

The course can't promise a timescale. How quickly a skill comes is down to the keeper and the work he puts in. What you get is a clear basis for every change you make to his work. It makes no promise about a keeper's career or your next job.

Will clubs and federations recognise it?

What about senior keepers?

Keepers in the adult game have their own ISSPF course. This one is about young keepers.
