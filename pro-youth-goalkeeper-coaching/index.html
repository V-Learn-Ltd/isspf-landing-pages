Why do I need this?

If you coach goalkeepers, you've probably pieced your coaching together yourself. There's no shortage of drills. What's missing is a clear path for developing a young keeper that shows up in your next game.

  • Drills come without the why. Clips, reels and session-plan sites show you a drill, but rarely what it's building towards.
  • Most coaching courses cover the whole team. On a general youth course, the keeper is one position among many, so there's little time on how to develop a goalkeeper.
  • The keeper in front of you doesn't fit the plan. When your keeper's sharp in training but not on match day, or a skill won't stick, it's hard to know what to change.
  • Someone always asks why. A parent or head coach asks why your keeper's doing that work. Responding with "it's a good drill" doesn't answer it.
  • The cost shows up late. A keeper can do the wrong work for seasons and still look fine. By the time it shows, they've moved up the age groups, and it's too late to trace where it went wrong.

This course gives you the whole path: drills and sessions from professional goalkeeping coaches, with the why behind them, so you know what each keeper needs next and can explain it to anyone who asks.

This course enables you to:

  • Plan what each young keeper you coach should be working on now
  • Coach the decisions a keeper makes before the save: reading the situation, choosing a position and picking the action, not just the technique of the stop
  • Take each skill from the basics to the finer detail and into the game, developing your keeper further as they get it right more often
  • Build skills that hold up in matches, with teammates, pressure and a wet pitch around them
  • Decide when a keeper trains alone and when with the team
  • Coach the keeper's role in attack, defence and transition
  • Develop a keeper who plays out against a high press, with the weaker foot as a real option
  • Prepare a keeper who's sharp in training for the pressure of a match
  • Analyse a keeper's game to find the real cause of a goal before you work on the touch
  • Know what to do when the same mistake keeps coming back, without damaging their confidence
  • Support young keepers off the pitch, including their nutrition as they grow

Overview

Know what each young goalkeeper you coach should be working on, how to develop them, and how to build skills that hold up in their matches.

The course covers the whole job: your role as their coach, their long-term development, how skills are learned and progressed, training alone and with the team, playing out, handling pressure, analysis, and nutrition as they grow. Every module is about the decisions you make with the keepers in front of you, whether that's one keeper in your child's team or an academy squad.

Most drills and tutorials teach the technique of stopping a shot. This course also develops what comes before it: how a keeper reads the situation, takes up position and decides what to do.

Anyone can show you the drill. We make sure you can explain it.

Who it's for

  • You coach young goalkeepers, in your child's team, across a club's age groups or in an academy.
  • You want to know what this keeper needs next, and why.
  • You want your keepers' training to show up in their matches.

It works one keeper and one skill at a time, so it fits a single keeper as well as a squad.

Outcomes

  • A plan for every keeper. You'll know what each young keeper should be working on, whether you coach one keeper or a squad.
  • Clear signals for the next step. How often a keeper gets it right tells you whether to stay with the basics, add the finer detail or bring the game in.
  • Skills that hold up in matches. Each skill moves from the basics into game situations a piece at a time, so training looks more like match day.
  • A keeper who plays their part in the team. They know their job when the team has the ball and when it hasn't, and they can play out under pressure.
  • An answer when anyone asks why. When your keeper, a parent or a head coach asks why they're working on something, you can say where that skill is and what comes next.

Course modules

Taught by seven professional goalkeeping coaches and two performance specialists.

Module 1: The role of the goalkeeper coach

Fraser StewartHas been head of goalkeeping at Kilmarnock and worked in coach education at the Scottish FA

You'll learn what it means to be a specialist goalkeeping coach, and what to weigh up for your keeper in training and on match day.

Module 2: Training Design & Application in Youth Development (LTAD)

Alex Segovia VilchezHas led performance at Singapore's national development centre

You'll learn a model for developing physical qualities, where mechanical and functional activities fit, and how exercise progresses through youth development.

Module 3: Individual development of the goalkeeper within a team environment

Ivan KovčićHas headed Dinamo Zagreb's academy goalkeeping programme

You'll learn when a keeper should train alone and when with the team, and how to spot their problems in football situations. You'll also get a simple test for what to work on: what keeps happening, not a one-off.

Module 4: Nutritional Support for the Youth Soccer Player: Maturation Status & LTAD

Jack ChristopherHas been head of performance support in Chelsea's academy

You'll learn how nutrition supports a young player through each phase of their development, as they grow and mature.

Module 5: Youth Goalkeeper Development: Long Term Development

Eduardo CachuloHas worked in NEOM's goalkeeping academy and with Real Valladolid's senior goalkeepers

You'll learn where the goalkeeper coach fits in a club's technical department, what marks success in the position, and how a young keeper makes the move from youth to senior football.

Module 6: Goalkeeping Expectations: Roles of the GK within the function of the game

Jeff RichardsonHas coached academy goalkeepers at Brentford

You'll learn how to coach the keeper's job when the team attacks, defends and transitions, including communication and the key situations they have to deal with.

Module 7: Skill Acquisition in Goalkeeping: Developing technical proficiency for situational effectiveness

Daniel Tumelty-BevanHas led academy goalkeeping at Birmingham City

You'll learn how a keeper learns a new skill, and when to use repetition or practice that looks like the game. How often they get it right becomes your signal to take them to the next step.

Module 8: Goalkeeper Coaching Analysis: Developmental Approach to Goalkeeping

Graeme SmithPlayed the game professionally

You'll learn which types of goalkeeper analysis are used in youth football, and how they shape a goalkeeping curriculum. In one example, a goal that looks like a lost header turns out to come from the keeper's positioning and judgement of the cross.

Module 9: Managing Pressure: Emotion, Anxiety and Coping, Part A

Jeff Richardson

You'll learn how emotion and anxiety affect a keeper, how they see a big moment, and how to prepare them mentally before a game. It's for the keeper who's sharp in training and different on match day.

Module 10: Goalkeeper Distribution in Academy Practice and Game Situations: FC Barcelona

Dani OrtizHas coached goalkeepers in FC Barcelona's academy

You'll learn how FC Barcelona's academy builds distribution: the keeper and outfielders beating a high press together, the weaker foot as a real option, and keepers rating their own passes like a traffic light. You'll see one skill built step by step, from the basics into the game.

LTAD stands for long-term athletic development: how a young player's training builds over the years.

Frequently asked questions

I coach my child's team, with one keeper. Is this for me?

Yes. It's for anyone who coaches young goalkeepers, from a parent with one keeper to an academy squad. The coaches and specialists who teach it have worked at professional clubs and in national programmes, but what they teach works one keeper and one skill at a time. So it fits a single keeper as well as a group.

I'm not a sports scientist. Will I follow it?

Yes. It's taught by goalkeeping coaches and performance specialists, about the decisions you make with your keepers. The modules on pressure and nutrition sit alongside the goalkeeping ones: pressure shapes how a keeper performs on match day, and nutrition supports them as they grow. If you can watch a keeper and count what they get right, you can use it.

Isn't there plenty of this on YouTube?

There are plenty of drill clips, and some are good. A clip shows you one drill. It can't tell you whether your keeper is ready for it, what comes next or why. Neither can it give you a clear development pathway for your young keepers. This course gives you all of it: drills, sessions and a development plan from professional goalkeeping coaches, and how to choose, progress and explain them for the keeper in front of you.

How soon will I see a difference?

In your next session. You'll be coaching with the methods of professional goalkeeping coaches and performance specialists from the top level of the game: the academies of FC Barcelona, Chelsea, Brentford, Birmingham City and Dinamo Zagreb, Kilmarnock's goalkeeping department and Real Valladolid's senior goalkeepers. What you learn goes straight into your sessions: what each keeper should be working on, how to progress their skills and when to take them to the next step. Every session gets a clear purpose, and each keeper develops at their own pace.
